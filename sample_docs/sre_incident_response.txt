Site reliability engineering treats reliability as a measurable feature. A Service Level Indicator (SLI) is a measurement of service behavior, such as the fraction of requests that succeed or the 99th percentile latency. A Service Level Objective (SLO) is the target for that indicator, for example 99.9 percent of requests succeeding over 30 days. A Service Level Agreement (SLA) is the contractual promise to customers, usually looser than the internal SLO.

The error budget is the amount of unreliability an SLO permits. A 99.9 percent availability SLO over a 30-day month allows roughly 43 minutes of downtime. While budget remains, teams can ship features and take risks. When the budget is exhausted, the team pauses risky releases and focuses on reliability work.

During an incident, one person takes the role of incident commander. The commander coordinates the response and makes decisions, but does not usually debug directly. Separate people handle communication with stakeholders and hands-on investigation. Incidents are often classified by severity, where the highest severity means a major customer-facing outage and lower levels mean limited impact.

Two common metrics are mean time to detect (MTTD), how long it takes to notice a problem, and mean time to resolve (MTTR), how long it takes to fix it. After an incident, the team writes a blameless postmortem. The goal is to understand the system and process failures that allowed the problem, not to blame an individual. A good postmortem lists a timeline, the root causes, what went well, and concrete action items with owners.
